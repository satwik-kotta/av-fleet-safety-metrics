Measuring autonomous driving performance from California's public filings

Every company testing self driving cars in California files its disengagements, mileage and collisions with the DMV. This page turns the 2021 to 2025 filings into fleet metrics with honest uncertainty, labels why each disengagement happened, and flags when a trend is really a change in how a company reports. Miles per disengagement, with 95% intervals

Company years with at least 100,000 autonomous miles. Log scale. The whiskers are exact Poisson intervals, so a year with few events shows how little it can prove.

Why disengagements happen

Share of each company's disengagements by the part of the stack (or the outside world) named as the cause, from rules on the free text.

How good is a cause classifier, really?

Same model, two evaluations. A random split of events leaks identical boilerplate sentences into the test set. Holding out whole companies does not.

Collisions: autonomous vs conventional mode

Share of DMV collision reports by collision type, 2014 to 2024, for the same test vehicles in each mode.

Zoox disengagements by driving situation

Share of Zoox's disengagements (2021 to 2025) whose cause text mentions each situation. One event can mention several.

Drift monitor on the filings

For each company, how much the cause mix moved from one year to the next (PSI) and how many of the year's cause sentences were new wording. A large shift with mostly new wording is a reporting change, not a driving change.
